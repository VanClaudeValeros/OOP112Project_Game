# OOP112 Project: Coins & Poker

## Game Overview
**Coins & Poker** is a hybrid Java-based game that combines elements of a coin slot machine and classic poker mechanics. The game blends randomness and strategy, offering players a dynamic experience where luck influences outcomes, but decision-making remains essential.

## Core Concept
At its core, the game revolves around two interconnected systems:

### Coin Slot System (Primary Attack Mechanism)
The coin slot acts as the main driver of action in the game. Each spin produces randomized results that determine the strength, type, or effectiveness of a player's attack. This introduces unpredictability, ensuring that no two turns are exactly the same.

### Poker Card System (Buffs and Debuffs)
Cards function as modifiers that enhance or hinder gameplay. Players draw and utilize poker hands to gain advantages such as increased damage, defensive boosts, or special effects. Certain hands may also apply penalties or debuffs, adding risk and complexity.

## Gameplay Mechanics

### Turn-Based Flow
Players take turns spinning the coin slot and applying card effects strategically.

### Randomized Outcomes
The slot machine determines base actions, while cards influence how those actions are modified.

### Strategic Decision-Making
Players must decide when to use or conserve cards, balancing immediate benefits with long-term survival.

### Buff and Debuff System
Poker hands trigger a variety of effects, including:
- Damage multipliers  
- Defensive shields  
- Status effects (e.g., stun, weaken, amplify)

## Objective
The goal is to defeat the opponent or survive longer by effectively combining slot outcomes with card-based strategies. Success depends on adapting to randomness while making calculated decisions.

## Technical Highlights
- Developed using **Java** with object-oriented programming principles  
- Modular class design (e.g., `Player`, `Card`, `SlotMachine`, `Effect`)  
- Randomization logic for slot spins and card draws  
- Extensible system for adding new cards, effects, and mechanics
